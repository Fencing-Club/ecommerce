# Copacker implementation contract

## Agreed product

Copacker is a multi-organization buyer/supplier portal. WorkOS authenticates users; database memberships and explicit buyer/supplier relationships authorize every record. Supplier organizations can work with multiple buyers. One PO has one destination. Partial shipments reserve quantities independently. Initial route: supplier to AWD USA; AWD China, FBA, and forwarders remain destination types but cannot create Amazon shipments until their implementation is verified.

PO quantities and prices are per individual piece. Pack size explicitly converts pieces to Amazon sellable units. Suppliers may propose supplier SKU and price changes, never buyer SKU/product identity changes. Commercial changes require buyer approval, update the PO, retain previous revisions, and sync approved supplier SKU to Shopify variant custom.supplier_sku. Shopify unit cost writeback is an explicit buyer choice with currency and pack conversion checks. An official commercial invoice PDF is mandatory.

Supplier submits their carton identifiers, contents, cm/kg measurements, ship-from contact/address, and local ready-for-pickup date. Server validation and Amazon eligibility precede shipment creation. Available Amazon carton labels are retrieved and mapped to supplier carton numbers. AGL booking is performed by the buyer in Amazon; Copacker waits for buyer-uploaded shipping labels. Readiness is not a booked pickup appointment. Released supplier documents include packing list, product labels, carton/shipping labels, and Packing Checklist. Shipment corrections invalidate affected documents and use supported update/replacement paths, never silently reuse labels.

## Architecture

- Next.js App Router and shadcn components; server-authorized actions/routes.
- Railway PostgreSQL; Drizzle migrations, transactional quantity reservations and immutable PO revisions.
- Private Railway S3 bucket; all original and generated documents in one store. Authenticated downloads, no public object URLs.
- Encrypted tenant credentials, expiring single-use OAuth state, exact-email supplier invitations.
- PostgreSQL durable jobs for Amazon, notifications, and Shopify writes. Creation timeouts are uncertain outcomes requiring reconciliation, never blind create retries.
- Reuse pure Aisle packing and label identity concepts; do not reuse local selected account or Windows credential storage as SaaS authorization.

## AI purchase-order import

Requested behavior: alongside manual catalog selection, a buyer uploads a supplier PO or invoice and receives an editable PO populated by an agent. The user reviews and corrects rows before saving. The import backend is implemented; the editor and live provider acceptance remain pending. Preserve the concurrent design workstream's components and styles.

**User workflow**

1. Choose Import with AI while creating a PO; select the supplier and destination, or complete those fields during review. Accept PDF initially, including scanned PDFs. Image and spreadsheet support must be explicit follow-up tasks rather than silently accepted unsupported files.
2. Upload into the existing private Railway document store. Create a tenant-owned import record and enqueue backend work; return immediately with an import ID. Show uploading, reading document, matching products, and ready for review states. Refreshing must resume the same import.
3. Present a Shopify-style editable line-item table in the PO editor: catalog product/variation, buyer SKU, supplier SKU, quantity and unit, pieces per sellable set, unit price and currency, line amount, and any issue needing review. Show the original source document alongside or through an accessible preview link.
4. Keep unmatched and ambiguous lines visible, with candidate products and a reason. The buyer can choose another product, search again, correct values, add or remove lines, and resolve missing fields. Do not silently drop lines, pick a similarly named variation, or create new Shopify products.
5. Save through the existing server-authorized PO creation path only after review. Saving re-fetches catalog identity and validates selected variants, tenant connections, currency, quantities, pack conversion and totals. Save creates a draft PO; the existing Submit PO action remains separate and sends the supplier email.

**Extraction and matching contract**

- Use OpenRouter model `anthropic/claude-opus-5.5`, verified against its official model page on 2026-09-25. No silent substitute model. Uploaded document content is data, never authority to change tools, accounts, recipients or instructions.
- Preserve source line description, source SKU, quantity, quantity unit, unit price, currency, line total, and page/row reference separately from normalized PO values. Unknown values remain null. Prices use validated decimal conversion and integer minor units in saved PO data, never floating-point total reconciliation.
- Match exact buyer SKU and supplier SKU first, then product name/variation and Amazon identity. A supplier SKU can identify multiple variants or products; exact text alone is insufficient when multiple candidates exist. Search all relevant catalog pages, and expose incomplete search results instead of asserting no match.
- Shopify is the canonical selected product/variation and buyer SKU source. Amazon supplies the connected seller's SKU, ASIN, FNSKU and pack/listing evidence for Amazon destinations. A model-proposed ID is accepted only after backend verification against that organization's catalog.
- PO quantities and prices remain per piece. Explicitly distinguish cartons, pieces and multi-packs. Preserve the original pricing basis and show the conversion; a missing or conflicting pack size requires review. Do not replace invoice prices with Shopify unit costs.
- Capture freight, packing charges, duties, taxes and discounts as separate document adjustments, not product rows. Show subtotal, adjustments and document grand total with any discrepancy. Extend PO totals/PDF generation to support reviewed adjustments before claiming invoices with these charges are fully imported.

## Inventory and incoming stock

This section defines the next implementation slices. It does not claim that Copacker currently reads warehouse stock or confirms receipts. Existing order workflow responses expose ordered, shipment-assigned and unassigned pieces for a single PO; those are operational quantities, not available inventory.

### Requirements and quantity rules

- Keep each buyer organization, connected merchant account, Shopify variant and destination distinct. Shopify variant IDs identify products within their store; buyer SKU, supplier SKU, ASIN and FNSKU are matching attributes, not globally unique primary keys. Do not merge ambiguous matches or sum stock across organizations.
- Keep Shopify locations, FBA marketplaces and AWD destinations separate. Fencing Club holds its own online-sales stock; Amazon FBA and AWD hold Amazon stock. An Amazon offer fulfilled from Fencing Club does not establish a need to stock FBA. Respect a configured zero Amazon stock target. Do not hard-code this business's location names into other organizations.
- Authoritative physical inventory: Amazon supplies FBA and AWD quantities. Shopify supplies stock at explicitly selected merchant locations, including stock available to fulfill Amazon FBM orders. A Shopify Amazon integration location can mirror the same Amazon inventory: retain its source observations, but exclude it from merchant stock and combined availability. Never add that mirror to direct Amazon inventory or use its incoming count as additional inbound stock.
- During inventory setup, buyers classify Shopify locations by stable store/location ID as merchant stock, Amazon mirror, excluded, or unresolved. Names are display labels, never automatic mapping keys. Amazon mirror associations identify the connected seller and applicable FBA/AWD scope only when verified; a generic Amazon location does not prove which pool it represents. Unresolved locations remain visible separately and cannot contribute to consolidated stock or restock recommendations. A missing/stale Amazon snapshot stays unknown; do not silently substitute a Shopify mirror. Location changes or reconnection require mapping review when identity changes.

- Show PO quantities in individual pieces and retain pack size for conversion to sellable units. Stock APIs can report sellable units; retain the source unit and require a verified pack conversion before combining with PO pieces. Unknown conversion stays unknown, never one by default.
- A draft PO is not an incoming-stock commitment. Submitted POs represent ordered pieces. Submitted packing reserves pieces using the existing `reservedPieces` policy; packing drafts and cancelled shipments do not reserve. Failed or uncertain Amazon creation still reserves its submitted quantities until the existing workflow resolves it.
- Partition each submitted PO line into unassigned pieces and assigned pieces. Assigned pieces can be broken down by shipment status, but never added again to the PO quantity. Show over-allocation as a discrepancy instead of hiding it behind a zero remaining balance.
- Supplier-reported collection, released labels and a ready-for-pickup date are not warehouse receipts, available stock or a confirmed arrival date. Closing a PO is not proof of receipt. The first slice must label these facts exactly and exclude closed POs from the open-order view without inventing a received quantity.
- Read stock and inbound data through authenticated provider APIs only. Preserve source, observation time, freshness and incomplete/error state. A failed refresh must not replace known stock with zero or present a partial page as a complete snapshot. Refresh is manual initially; scheduled syncing is deferred.
- Shopify incoming quantities may already include Shopify POs. Never add those counts to Copacker PO quantities without an explicit shared record identity and reconciliation rule. Keep the two sources separately visible until that relationship can be established. Shopify PO API access is a future integration, not a reason to reconstruct POs from manually downloaded documents.
- Supplier lead time is configurable. Initially accept an explicit eight-week lead time for a report, as requested. Do not silently assign that default to all suppliers or infer an arrival date from the pickup-ready date.
- Case quantity belongs to the Shopify product, not the variant. Missing means loose-piece ordering; a positive value rounds an order to whole cases. Sellable-set size and case quantity are different concepts. Mixed-variation case allocation needs an explicit rule before recommending shared cases. Standard single foil/epee cords use 160 pieces per case; other ranges must not inherit that setting automatically.
- No historical stock ledger, inventory writes, Finaloop integration, automatic purchasing or restock automation is included in these slices.

### Current snapshot storage and read contract

Implementation target, not a live API: a current stock snapshot is a complete bounded provider read for one buyer, connected account and source scope. It is not a transactionally frozen upstream ledger. Retain both collection start and finish timestamps to make that distinction visible. Do not add a new dependency or stock-history subsystem.

Use one `stock_snapshots` row per `(orgId, source, externalAccountId, scopeKey)`. Proposed fields:

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `orgId` | Buyer organization foreign key; every read and write includes it. |
| `source` | `shopify`, `fba`, or `awd`. |
| `externalAccountId` | Pinned Shopify domain or Amazon seller ID from the authenticated connection. Never supplied by the browser. |
| `scopeKey` | Shopify store-wide collection (`store`), one FBA marketplace ID, or supported AWD US network (`us`). Individual Shopify locations remain in rows; AWD totals are not fabricated warehouse balances. |
| `revision` | Monotonic integer advanced when a refresh is claimed. A finishing attempt may publish only while it owns this revision and the same connected account. |
| `status` | `idle`, `queued`, `running`, or `failed`; separate from whether a usable snapshot exists. |
| `requestedAt`, `attemptStartedAt`, `attemptFinishedAt` | Latest refresh attempt timestamps, nullable until the relevant transition. |
| `snapshotId`, `startedAt`, `observedAt` | UUID and start/end timestamps for the last complete published collection; all null before the first success. Failed attempts preserve them. |
| `data` | Validated source-specific JSON rows for the current complete snapshot, null before first success. An empty array is a successful complete empty collection, not an error substitute. |
| `errorCode` | Bounded application code for latest failure; never raw provider errors, tokens or documents. |

The initial bounded JSON snapshot avoids prematurely creating a stock ledger or row-indexing service. Respect provider collection limits and a serialized-payload cap; exceeding either fails the attempt without replacing the prior snapshot. Revisit row storage only when measured account size requires it. Do not persist partial pages. Concurrent refreshes reuse an active attempt through the existing durable job mechanism; expired leases use existing recovery. A previous worker losing ownership must not publish its result or mark the successor failed.

Normalize a discriminated union rather than forcing all providers into one misleading `available` balance:

- Shopify rows: location ID/name, variant ID, inventory item ID, buyer SKU (nullable), tracked flag, and signed nullable `available`, `onHand`, `incoming`, `committed`. Unit is `shopify_inventory_unit`; do not assume it is one physical piece. Missing states remain null, and untracked items remain explicitly untracked.
- FBA rows: marketplace, seller SKU, nullable ASIN/FNSKU/condition; nullable total, fulfillable, inbound-working/shipped/receiving, reserved, unfulfillable and researching quantities. Preserve supported subcategory breakdowns separately, never add them to their parent totals. Unit is `amazon_sellable_unit`.
- AWD rows: seller SKU; nullable inbound, on-hand, available-distributable, reserved-distributable and replenishment quantities. Unit is `amazon_sellable_unit`. An AWD stock row alone does not establish ASIN, FNSKU, Shopify identity or a receipt for a particular shipment.

Publish source identities first; matching to Shopify variants is a separate verified mapping, including pack conversion. Unknown or ambiguous mappings do not prevent viewing source stock, but exclude the row from piece-based restock totals. Do not infer pack size from SKU text, product names or a changed PO. Keep prior stock hidden when the active connection no longer matches its pinned account.

Planned routes:

- `POST /api/inventory/refresh`: buyer-only `{source, marketplaceId?}`. FBA requires a supported marketplace; other sources reject that field. Account and region come from the active tenant's validated connection and source rules. Return 202 with attempt identity; repeated requests reuse an active attempt. No external stock write or scheduled sync.
- `GET /api/inventory`: buyer-only source/scope, bounded page size and cursor. Return scope identity, `snapshotId`, collection timestamps, latest refresh state/error code, rows and next cursor. No snapshot means null snapshot metadata and unavailable quantities; never a zero-stock assertion.
- Cursors bind snapshot UUID, organization, account, scope and row offset. Validate against the current connection and snapshot on every page. Replacement yields 409 and asks the caller to restart; no historical snapshot retention just to support old cursors. Stable canonical row ordering is required within a published snapshot.

Freshness is elapsed time since `observedAt`, not an invented guarantee about upstream stock. The interface must show collection time and a failed latest refresh even while displaying the last successful data. A future freshness threshold is a product setting, not a reason to discard usable evidence silently. PO coverage remains a separate response; no stock/incoming total joins until receipt reconciliation is implemented.

Existing queries request `available`, `on_hand`, `incoming` and `committed`; signed values must be preserved. No merchant query was executed in this research.

FBA [getInventorySummaries](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/getinventorysummaries) is marketplace-scoped and supports one marketplace per request. Full reads omit SKU and `startDateTime` filters and request `details=true`. Incremental reads miss changes in inbound working/shipped/receiving quantities, so they cannot replace full snapshots. Page tokens expire after 30 seconds; consume promptly and abandon incomplete collections on failure. The [FBA API role matrix](https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/lang-en_EN/docs/fba-inventory-api) permits Amazon Fulfillment or Product Listing roles across NA, EU and FE. Copacker's current Amazon transport is fixed to NA; other regions require validated marketplace-to-endpoint selection before claiming support.

Amazon's [AWD API guide](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/amazon-warehousing-and-distribution-api) documents US-store availability, NA region and the Amazon Warehousing and Distribution role. The [listInventory operation](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/reference/listinventory) supports `details=SHOW`, up to 200 results, and empty pages with a continuation token; collection must continue until that token is absent. The [response model](https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/awd_2024-05-09-reference) distinguishes seller-to-AWD inbound units, on-hand units, available/reserved downstream replenishment and units in transit from AWD to FBA. Totals and breakdowns are not additive stock pools. This does not establish a China stock endpoint or individual warehouse balances; keep those unavailable until a supported contract is verified. These SKU-level totals also do not prove receipt of a particular Copacker shipment.
