# Analytics competitive context

Research baseline: 2026-10-16. Public product claims inform positioning, not verified performance or feature parity.

Sellerboard centers on Amazon profit visibility after fees, ads, returns and inventory costs. Its likely core audience
is operating sellers and small teams; this is an inference from positioning and order-volume pricing, not customer
demographic research. It explicitly serves brands, resellers and agencies, with a separate Shopify offering.
Sources: [Amazon product](https://sellerboard.com/), [agencies](https://sellerboard.com/agencies),
[resellers](https://sellerboard.com/en/reseller-workflow), [Shopify](https://sellerboard.io/).

Relevant competitive depth includes historical/batch costs, detailed product profit, returns and recovery analysis,
advertising tools, seller alerts and review requests. Its inventory offering also includes POs and inbound workflows;
having purchasing features alone is not a sufficient differentiator.
Sources: [inventory](https://sellerboard.com/inventory), [PPC](https://sellerboard.com/ppc),
[search analytics](https://sellerboard.com/search-terms).

Copacker already connects replenishment, supplier collaboration, POs, forwarders and FBA/AWD shipments with sales and
cost data. A promising initial audience is brands selling through Amazon and Shopify with recurring procurement and
logistics coordination. This is a positioning hypothesis to validate with customers.

The opportunity is to connect reliable product economics to purchasing and operational decisions. The accepted
[priorities](README.md) focus first on the financial data foundation; they do not require reproducing every competitor
feature. Customer-ID ingestion and repeat-customer calculations already exist in the current code and must not be
described as entirely absent.
